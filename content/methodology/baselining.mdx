# Baseline expected infrastructure communication

A useful baseline is a reviewed dependency model, not a list of every flow ever observed. Treat each appliance as a host with clients, servers, identities, scheduled work, and trust relationships. Record the behavior the organization intends to permit, then investigate deviations instead of allowing yesterday's compromise to become tomorrow's baseline.

## Expected-dependency inventory

For every managed appliance and service, record:

1. **Device identity and role** — hostname, asset identifier, platform, owner, environment, and business function.
2. **Management addresses and interfaces** — dedicated management IPs, loopbacks, VRFs, zones, and paths.
3. **Approved initiators** — jump hosts, controllers, automation services, administrators, peers, and monitoring systems allowed to start a session.
4. **Approved destinations** — AAA, DNS, NTP, logging, configuration, update, directory, tunnel, and routing peers.
5. **Protocol, port, and encapsulation** — include negotiated or non-default ports and the expected security mode.
6. **Direction and device role** — state who initiates and whether the appliance acts as client, server, peer, forwarder, resolver, or tunnel endpoint.
7. **Plane and path** — management, control, or data plane; expected source interface, zone, next hop, and policy boundary.
8. **Identity and authorization** — service account, administrator group, peer identity, certificate, key, community, and permitted command or API scope.
9. **Timing and cadence** — continuous, event-driven, periodic, maintenance-only, or tied to a change ticket.
10. **Volume and fan-out** — expected bytes, packets, session duration, peer count, and burst behavior.
11. **Evidence and stewardship** — authoritative configuration source, telemetry sources, approving owner, review date, and expiration or revalidation date.

Inventory both directions independently. “SNMP is allowed” is incomplete: polling normally runs from an NMS to a router on UDP/161, while traps or informs run from the router to an NMS on UDP/162. A router originating UDP/161 toward many systems is a different behavior.

## Review deviations

A deviation is a question, not an automatic incident. Confirm the asset role, change record, observed initiator, source interface, NAT path, authenticated identity, and independent telemetry. Update the matrix only after the owner confirms the dependency is necessary, constrained, and observable.
